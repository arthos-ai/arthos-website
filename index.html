The problem

Knowledge drifts out of sync faster than anyone can keep up.

Decisions supersede earlier decisions. Plans, policies, and processes get revised, often somewhere other than where the original lives. Both versions stay in circulation, and neither says which one still holds.

Every change also has implications. When one fact changes, the facts and decisions built on it have to change too, but nothing records those relationships. So teams coordinate by hand or work from facts that are no longer true, and that shows up as cost, delays, rework, and customers getting mixed answers.

Why now

The problem isn't new. The stakes are.

People have always kept an organization's knowledge in line. Someone notices a document is out of date. Someone knows who to ask, or catches that two things don't line up. It was never efficient, but it mostly worked.

Now knowledge changes faster than people can track. Teams produce more plans, documents, and decisions than ever. AI tools read those same sources and act on them, without a person's judgment. So more work gets built on information that's no longer current. It travels further before anyone notices: into other teams' work, into what ships, and into what customers are told.

By the time it surfaces, fixing it costs far more than catching it would have.

Why existing tools fall short

Finding knowledge is solved. Keeping it correct is not.

Shared drives, wikis, and search tools are good at storing information and returning what matches a search. But they treat every document as if it stands on its own. They can tell you when a document was last edited. They can't tell you whether what's in it still holds, how it relates to everything else, or what's affected when it changes.

So even when you find an answer, you can't be sure it's still right.

The gap isn't finding knowledge. It's trusting it.

What Arthos is

A trusted knowledge layer your whole organization can rely on.

Arthos connects to the drives, wikis, and ticketing systems your teams already use. It extracts the policies, requirements, and decisions inside them. It keeps track of where each one came from, how it has changed, and what depends on it.

That changes how decisions get made.

Say your product team wants to shorten a warranty from two years to one. Before the change goes out, Arthos shows everything built on the current policy. That includes the support team's help articles, the sales contract template, the training guide, and the answers your chatbot gives. You can see which teams will need to act and what they'll need to change. Once you approve the change, Arthos takes care of the rest.

Arthos also flags when two sources disagree, while it's still easy to fix. Your team sees the evidence behind each version and decides which one holds.

The result is one place to get the current answer. Everyone works from the same facts, and so do your AI tools. Decisions start from the latest policy. The teams they affect know in advance. And less work gets redone after the fact.

Nothing gets migrated or replaced. Your sources stay where they are, and the knowledge inside them stays connected and current.
